Our approach

Partnership, before performance.

At Foxfire, horsemanship begins with communication, relationship, and respect for the individual. Both our horses and our humans come to us with diverse backgrounds, experiences, strengths, challenges, and goals, and good horsemanship means learning to understand the individual in front of you. Care team and volunteers handle horses with collaborative leadership. Riders, who are also always part of the care team, learn not to "make a horse perform a task," but to listen, communicate clearly, build trust, and develop partnerships over time. We believe those skills matter in the saddle, on the ground, and far beyond the barn.

Our training foundation

All of our riders are taught from a foundation of dressage-based flatwork, with an emphasis on developing correct position, balance, effective aids, and thoughtful communication between horse and rider. As riders progress, they may choose to pursue jumping when they, their parents, and their instructors or mentors agree that they are ready, but jumping is never a requirement. Some Foxfire riders choose to focus exclusively on dressage, and there is a lifetime of horsemanship, skill, and partnership to develop without ever leaving the ground.

Eventing

Eventing is an equestrian triathlon in which horse-and-rider partnerships compete in three distinct phases: dressage, cross-country, and show jumping. The sport tests far more than proficiency in three separate disciplines. It requires a horse to combine obedience and correct training with athleticism, endurance, courage, carefulness, and adaptability. Riders must develop their horses systematically so that the same partnership can produce precise, controlled work in the dressage arena, gallop boldly and efficiently across country, and return to the show-jumping ring with the balance and accuracy to leave the rails up. The result is one of the most comprehensive tests of horse and rider in equestrian sport.

The three phases

Dressage: Dressage provides the foundation for every phase of eventing. The test evaluates the quality of the horse's training through rhythm, suppleness, contact, impulsion, straightness, balance, and obedience, as well as the harmony and effectiveness of the partnership. Rather than simply performing a prescribed series of movements, horse and rider demonstrate the progressive development of the horse's way of going and the qualities that make the horse more rideable, adjustable, and athletic throughout the competition.

Cross-country: Cross-country is the defining endurance and bravery test of eventing. Horse and rider travel at speed over varied terrain while negotiating solid obstacles and technical questions that may include banks, ditches, water, combinations, and changes of elevation. A successful round requires courage and trust, but also balance, adjustability, accuracy, stamina, and sound judgment. The rider must establish an efficient rhythm, choose appropriate lines and speeds, and prepare the horse for each question without unnecessarily disrupting the gallop.

Show jumping: Show jumping tests the horse and rider's ability to negotiate a course of obstacles accurately within an allowed time while leaving the fences standing. Success requires balance, adjustability, impulsion, carefulness, and an effective, economical track. In eventing, the phase also demonstrates whether the horse's training allows the rider to maintain rhythm and control while producing the power and technique necessary to jump cleanly.

A familiar face

Boss Man

Our surprisingly elegant, unexpectedly athletic, resident class clown and reigning Chubbiest Showman! Boss is everyone's go-to for a good laugh, always in your pocket and up for a cuddle. The pudgy exterior fools people: riders and spectators are always surprised by his lovely movement in dressage and his athleticism over jumps. He's taught plenty of riders the basics and more.

Our horses come to us from many different backgrounds. Some have been well loved and cared for, but their families encountered changes or challenges in life; others have experienced neglect, inadequate care, or even abuse. Whatever their history, each horse is treated as an individual, with thoughtfully tailored nutrition, appropriate veterinary and farrier care, safe and knowledgeable handling, and training designed around that horse's physical and emotional needs. Our volunteers, team members, and riders become part of that process, learning how consistent care, patient communication, and good horsemanship help horses become healthy, confident partners.
